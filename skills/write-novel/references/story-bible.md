# Story Bible

Use this reference when a fiction project needs durable memory, long-form planning, or continuity control.

## Minimal Story Bible

Create or update these sections:

- **Logline**: protagonist + desire + obstacle + stakes + distinctive hook.
- **Genre promise**: what readers expect to repeatedly receive.
- **Core engine**: the recurring source of conflict that can generate many chapters.
- **Theme question**: the moral or emotional question the story tests through action.
- **Ending direction**: the likely final choice, victory, loss, or transformation.
- **Protagonist**: public goal, private need, wound, false belief, strength, flaw, forbidden line, voice markers.
- **Opposition**: antagonist or pressure system, motive, resources, blind spot, escalation pattern.
- **Key relationships**: desire, debt, secret, conflict, tenderness, betrayal risk.
- **World rules**: magic/tech/social rules, costs, constraints, loopholes, consequences.
- **Timeline**: dated or ordered events, travel time, injuries, cooldowns, promises, deadlines.
- **Open threads**: mysteries, foreshadowing, vows, threats, debts, unresolved emotional beats.
- **Resolved threads**: what has been paid off and should not be reopened casually.

## Long-Form Discipline

Prefer constraints that generate story: deadlines, prices, rules, secrets, scarcity, public reputation, vows, institutions, geography, and relationships. Avoid solving major problems by adding unforeshadowed powers, coincidences, sudden stupidity, or unexplained rule changes.
